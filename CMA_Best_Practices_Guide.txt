              CMA ANALYSIS - BEST PRACTICES GUIDE FOR CREDIT OFFICERS
              Standard Framework for Credit Monitoring Accounts

1. WHAT IS A CMA?
================================================================================

CMA (Credit Monitoring Account) or CMA Statement is a structured analysis tool
used by commercial banks to evaluate the creditworthiness of borrowers before
loan disbursement.

Key Features:
- Consolidates historical financial data (typically 3 years)
- Projects future financials over loan tenure
- Analyzes debt servicing capacity
- Assesses risk and assigns credit rating
- Establishes financial covenants and monitoring framework

2. ESSENTIAL COMPONENTS OF A STANDARD CMA
================================================================================

A. BORROWER PROFILE SECTION
   • Legal name and registration details
   • Nature of business and industry classification
   • Business experience and track record
   • Ownership structure (proprietor/partner/director details)
   • Location and contact information
   • Key management personnel details

B. LOAN PARTICULARS
   • Loan amount and tenor
   • Purpose of loan (specific asset/working capital)
   • Rate of interest and repayment frequency
   • Security offered (collateral and guarantees)
   • Proposed disbursement schedule
   • Any special features (moratorium, step-up, etc.)

C. FINANCIAL STATEMENTS (3 YEARS HISTORICAL)
   • Income Statement (P&L Account)
     - Revenue/Sales
     - Cost of Goods Sold
     - Operating Expenses
     - EBITDA
     - Depreciation & Finance Costs
     - PBT & PAT

   • Balance Sheet
     - Total Assets (Current + Fixed)
     - Total Liabilities (Current + Long-term)
     - Equity/Net Worth
     - Key ratios (Current ratio, Debt-Equity, etc.)

   • Cash Flow Statement
     - Operating activities cash flow
     - Investing activities
     - Financing activities

D. FINANCIAL PROJECTIONS (LOAN TENURE)
   • Assumptions document (growth rates, margins, capex)
   • Projected P&L for loan tenure
   • Balance sheet projections
   • Cash flow projections
   • Depreciation schedules
   • Tax calculations

E. DEBT SERVICE ANALYSIS
   • Amortization schedule (year-wise)
   • Monthly/Quarterly EMI breakdown
   • Interest vs. Principal composition
   • Repayment capacity assessment
   • Residual cash flow analysis

F. FINANCIAL RATIOS
   • Profitability: Gross Profit Margin, EBITDA Margin, Net Profit Margin
   • Leverage: Debt-Equity, Debt-Asset, Interest Coverage
   • Liquidity: Current Ratio, Quick Ratio, Working Capital
   • Efficiency: Asset Turnover, Inventory Turnover, Receivable Days
   • Debt Service: DSCR, ICR, LTV

G. RISK ASSESSMENT
   • Business risk (industry, competition, regulatory)
   • Financial risk (profitability, cash flow, leverage)
   • Market risk (demand, pricing, customer concentration)
   • Operational risk (management capability, execution)

H. SECURITY ASSESSMENT
   • Collateral valuation and LTV calculation
   • Insurance coverage requirements
   • Registration and legal issues
   • Liquidation value vs. loan amount

I. FINANCIAL COVENANTS & MONITORING
   • Affirmative covenants (what borrower must do)
   • Negative covenants (what borrower must not do)
   • Key metrics to monitor (DSCR, margins, leverage)
   • Review frequency and triggers
   • Early warning signals

3. KEY FINANCIAL RATIOS FOR CMA ANALYSIS
================================================================================

A. DEBT SERVICE COVERAGE RATIO (DSCR)
   Formula: Annual Net Cash Inflow / Annual Debt Service Obligation

   Interpretation:
   - > 2.50x    : Excellent (Low risk)
   - 1.50-2.50x : Good (Acceptable)
   - 1.00-1.50x : Marginal (Review required)
   - < 1.00x    : Poor (Reject)

   Note: Minimum DSCR acceptance criterion is typically 1.50x for SME loans

B. INTEREST COVERAGE RATIO (ICR)
   Formula: EBIT / Annual Interest Expense

   Interpretation:
   - > 5.0x     : Excellent (Comfortable interest payment)
   - 2.5-5.0x   : Good
   - 2.0-2.5x   : Acceptable
   - < 2.0x     : Weak (Liquidity concern)

   Note: Indicates how many times operating profit covers interest obligations

C. LOAN-TO-VALUE RATIO (LTV)
   Formula: Loan Amount / Collateral Value (or Annual Revenue)

   Typical Benchmarks:
   - Asset-based lending: LTV < 70-80%
   - Revenue-based lending: LTV < 50%
   - Vehicle loans: LTV < 100% (typically 80-90%)

   Note: Lower LTV provides safety margin against collateral depreciation

D. DEBT-EQUITY RATIO
   Formula: Total Debt / Total Equity

   Interpretation:
   - < 1.0      : Conservative (Low leverage)
   - 1.0-2.0    : Moderate (Acceptable)
   - > 2.0      : High (Risky leverage)

E. CURRENT RATIO
   Formula: Current Assets / Current Liabilities

   Interpretation:
   - > 2.0      : Strong liquidity
   - 1.5-2.0    : Acceptable
   - 1.0-1.5    : Tight liquidity
   - < 1.0      : Liquidity crisis

F. PROFITABILITY MARGINS
   - Gross Profit Margin = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue
   - EBITDA Margin = EBITDA / Revenue
   - Net Profit Margin = PAT / Revenue

   Note: Compare with industry benchmarks for context

4. ASSUMPTIONS FOR FINANCIAL PROJECTIONS
================================================================================

When creating forward-looking CMA statements, use realistic assumptions:

REVENUE ASSUMPTIONS:
- Conservative historical growth rate
- Industry growth trend analysis
- Market size and competitive position
- Customer concentration and retention
- Contractual revenue commitments
- Typical ranges: 0-15% annual growth for stability; 10-20% for high-growth sectors

COST ASSUMPTIONS:
- Variable costs as % of revenue (COGS, distribution, etc.)
- Fixed costs (rent, salaries, utilities)
- Historical cost trends and inflation factors
- Efficiency improvements with scale
- Impact of input price changes

WORKING CAPITAL ASSUMPTIONS:
- Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) - receivable collection period
- Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) - inventory holding period
- Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) - supplier credit period
- Working capital financing needs

CAPEX & DEPRECIATION:
- Asset replacement schedule
- Technology upgrade requirements
- Capacity expansion needs
- Useful life estimates for depreciation
- Residual value assumptions

TAX ASSUMPTIONS:
- Corporate tax rate (typically 25-30% for Indian companies)
- Tax holidays or incentives (if any)
- VAT/GST implications
- Dividend distribution tax
- Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT) if applicable

5. ASSESSING DEBT SERVICING CAPACITY
================================================================================

Step 1: Calculate Available Cash Flow
- Start with Operating Cash Flow (Cash from operations)
- Add: Non-cash expenses (Depreciation, Amortization)
- Less: Mandatory payments (taxes, existing debt)
- Equals: Free Cash Flow Available for New Debt Service

Step 2: Calculate Required Debt Service
- Annual EMI (Principal + Interest) for proposed loan
- Existing loan obligations
- Other mandatory payables
- Total Annual Debt Service

Step 3: Calculate DSCR
- DSCR = Available Cash Flow / Total Debt Service
- Apply minimum threshold (typically 1.50x)
- Calculate coverage buffer

Step 4: Stress Test
- Reduce revenue by 10-20%
- Increase costs by 5-10%
- Recalculate DSCR
- Ensure minimum threshold maintained

Step 5: Cash Flow Over Loan Tenure
- Project year-wise DSCR
- Identify weakest years
- Ensure no year falls below minimum threshold
- Plan for early repayment if cash permits

6. DETERMINING LOAN QUANTUM
================================================================================

Maximum Loan Amount should be determined by:

Formula: Maximum Loan = (Available Annual Cash Flow / Debt Service per Re.)
         × Minimum DSCR Requirement

Example Calculation:
- Available Annual Cash Flow = Rs. 50 lakhs
- For 5-year loan at 12% ROI: Annual EMI/Rs. 1 lakh loan ≈ Rs. 0.275
- Minimum DSCR = 1.50x
- Maximum Loan = (50,00,000 / 0.275) × 1.50 = ~Rs. 27.3 crore

Constraints to consider:
- Collateral value and LTV limits
- Borrower's equity contribution (typically 20-30%)
- Lender's overall credit exposure
- Purpose-based lending limits
- Industry-specific exposure caps

7. FINANCIAL COVENANTS FRAMEWORK
================================================================================

QUANTITATIVE COVENANTS (with specific thresholds):
- Minimum DSCR: 1.50x annually
- Maximum Debt-Equity: 2.0x
- Minimum Current Ratio: 1.25x
- Maximum Interest Coverage: < 2.0x (warning)
- Minimum Net Worth growth: 5% annually

QUALITATIVE COVENANTS:
- Quarterly financial statements submission
- Annual auditor's certificate
- Compliance certificates (tax, regulatory)
- Regular operational updates
- Immediate notification of material events

TRIGGER EVENTS (for enhanced scrutiny):
- DSCR falls below 1.50x
- Interest payment delay > 30 days
- Default on any covenant
- Significant business disruption
- Change in ownership/management
- Regulatory action against borrower

8. MONITORING & REVIEW PROCESS
================================================================================

FREQUENCY OF REVIEW:
- Monthly: Payment compliance, basic health check
- Quarterly: Financial performance, ratio analysis
- Half-yearly: Covenant compliance, business updates
- Annually: Full review, rerating, refinancing assessment

MONITORING TOOLS:
1. Financial Analysis
   - P&L tracking (Actuals vs. Projections)
   - Ratio trend analysis
   - DSCR recalculation
   - Working capital assessment

2. Operational Monitoring
   - Production/sales volume trends
   - Capacity utilization
   - Customer concentration changes
   - Competitive position
   - Management changes

3. Cash Flow Tracking
   - Bank statement reconciliation
   - Major inflows/outflows analysis
   - Working capital cycle changes
   - Investment/disposal activities

4. Collateral Monitoring
   - Insurance validity checks
   - Valuation updates
   - Registration status
   - Liquidation value assessment

9. EARLY WARNING SYSTEM
================================================================================

RED FLAGS (Immediate action required):
✗ 2+ EMI defaults in past 12 months
✗ DSCR drops below 1.25x
✗ Continuous quarterly losses
✗ Major customer loss (>20% revenue)
✗ Regulatory/legal action
✗ Management departures
✗ Covenant breaches
✗ Collateral damage/loss

YELLOW FLAGS (Enhanced monitoring):
⚠ Single EMI default/delay
⚠ DSCR between 1.25-1.50x
⚠ Margin compression (>5% deterioration)
⚠ Revenue growth slowdown
⚠ Increased financial leverage
⚠ Working capital deterioration
⚠ Change in business strategy

10. COMMON MISTAKES IN CMA ANALYSIS
================================================================================

FINANCIAL MISTAKES:
× Using only PAT (ignoring EBITDA) for cash flow analysis
× Ignoring tax impact on projections
× Extrapolating historical growth without validation
× Not adjusting working capital requirements
× Underestimating capex needs
× Ignoring owner distributions/drawings

ANALYSIS MISTAKES:
× Relying solely on financial statements (no operational checks)
× Not conducting industry benchmarking
× Ignoring competitive threats
× Missing seasonal/cyclical patterns
× Weak collateral valuations
× Overlooking related party transactions

COVENANT MISTAKES:
× Setting unrealistic DSCR thresholds
× Vague covenants with no measurement metric
× Missing early warning triggers
× No escalation mechanism
× Inadequate monitoring frequency
× Poor documentation

RISK ASSESSMENT MISTAKES:
× Overreliance on historical performance
× Ignoring macroeconomic factors
× Weak stress testing
× Not updating ratings periodically
× Inadequate pricing for risk
× No contingency plans

PRE-APPROVAL:
☑ Verified financial statements (audited preferred)
☑ 3-year historical analysis completed
☑ Industry benchmarking done
☑ Collateral valuation obtained
☑ CIBIL/credit report reviewed

☑ Review schedule established
☑ Early warning signals defined
☑ Monitoring KPIs identified
